# Proposed impact-statement revision

This file contains a proposed replacement for the `impact-statement` field in
`usrse2026.qmd`. The accepted manuscript source remains unchanged; integrate
this text manually after review.

```text
Scientific programming workflows are becoming more complex, while existing
support mechanisms often remain poorly matched to that complexity. We see
Quarto Manuals as a practical contribution to this problem and as directly
aligned with the interests of the US-RSE community. Research software
engineers are often responsible not only for building software, but also for
making workflows reproducible, maintainable, usable, and inspectable by
others. Quarto Manuals support that work through executable, operator-facing
procedures that expose workflow state, transitions, consequential human
judgment, and verification boundaries. Mechanical execution may be automated,
while the decisions that determine a scientific or computational run remain
visible.

This contribution has three main highlights. First, it promotes technical
resources for documenting and delivering complex scientific workflows. Second,
it treats workflow design, execution, and reproducible handoff as first-class
research software engineering artifacts. Third, it provides a bounded interface
through which human-authored operational intent and agent or software execution
can be scoped, inspected, and verified. Quarto Manuals complement conventional
software engineering tools; they do not replace workflow engines, test suites,
or ordinary scripts, and they do not make AI systems reliable by themselves.

Ultimately, Quarto Manuals aim to make scientific programming workflows more
reproducible, transparent, and maintainable while preserving expert judgment
and clear ownership of computational decisions.
```
